TheFly

Searching the Drosophila optic-lobe connectome for the circuits behind contrast gain control, and asking what they do to the contrast response function (CRF).

How the pieces fit

Everything here is computed from published data: no new experiments. The physiology to compare against is Drews et al. 2020 (surround contrast normalization in the medulla) and the Silies lab's luminance gain control work.

The Dm3 orientation subconnectome, simulated

Every Dm3p/q/v and TmY9q/TmY9q⊥/TmY4 neuron of the right optic lobe, wired with its real FlyWire synapse counts. Columnar inputs carry rectified ON (Mi1, Tm3, Mi4, C3) or OFF (Tm1, Tm2, Tm4, Tm9, L2, L3) contrast; signs follow transmitter (ACh +, GABA/Glu −). Stimuli are static gratings on the eye's column lattice; responses are the peak over grating phase. The model runs in your browser.

Cutting subconnectomes

Edges removed from the trained networks before simulating (no retraining). "Lateral inhibition" cuts every inhibitory connection between different columns (photoreceptor and lamina outputs spared); "lateral excitation" the excitatory equivalent.

Adding the missing denominator

Dm17, Dm1 and Dm15 are grafted onto trained flyvis networks as smooth pools over the eye. Their input weights, output targets and spatial extent all come from FlyWire. Each pool is driven by its inputs' depolarization relative to grey, so the grey state is untouched. It then either divides its targets' drive (shunting toward the operating point) or subtracts a matched current. Gain g = 1 halves the main target's drive under a full-field, full-contrast grating.
